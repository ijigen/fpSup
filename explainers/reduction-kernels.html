What the binning actually does

Every description of the fp's reduction modes — ours included — says “sensor 2×2” and “sensor 3×3”. Seven frames of one scene say otherwise. Vertically the sensor really does average rows. Horizontally it never averages anything: it interpolates. And the ÷3 mode bins only two rows out of every three.

  • 7 frames one scene, one exposure
  • 3 methods that must agree
  • 2×2 = 3.2 photodiodes, not 4
Method
Least-squares recovery of the reduction kernel from raw frames, checked against two measurements that share none of its assumptions: photon-transfer noise statistics, and the sub-pixel geometry of the two green planes.
Checked against
A control pair shot after the camera was taken off the tripod and refocused; synthetic frames built with known kernels and pushed through the same pipeline; and the firmware's own merge-tuple table from Every setting on the sensor, which agrees row for row.
Not from the firmware
Nothing here was read out of a disassembly. It is what one camera did, which is a different kind of claim from what the firmware would do — and it cannot say which block applied a kernel, only what reached the card. See what is still open.

The short version

Three things, if you read nothing else:

  1. Vertical is real binning. Horizontal is not.

    The vertical kernel is an exact equal-weight average of two same-colour rows, and it leaves a fingerprint no resampler can fake: the output rows come out unevenly spaced. The horizontal kernel has unequal weights that mirror between even and odd output columns — the signature of resampling onto a uniform grid. Charge combination cannot produce that.

  2. The ÷3 mode bins two rows, not three

    OG2K throws away one same-colour row in every three. That is not an inference from the pixels alone: the firmware's own merge tuple for those eight modes is (3,2,3,3), and the lone 2 is the vertical tap count. The pixels and the ROM table say the same thing.

  3. The cheapest large win is not in the sensor

    Feeding the ÷2 sensor mode into the resampler at ×1.5 beats a textbook true 3×3 on aliasing and on noise — using only blocks that already ship. The FHD mode is that cascade already.

How it was measured

One frame per mode of the same scene, same lens, same exposure: ISO 400, 1/25 s, f/3.5, 25p, Ver.5.02. A guitar amplifier, because the grille cloth is a dense regular weave — the worst case for any reduction, and therefore the one that separates the candidates.

OG3K and OG2K cannot live on the same card, so the session splits in two, with the camera remounted and refocused in between. That split turns out to matter:

Three measurements run on those frames. They share no assumptions, so they are allowed to disagree — and where they agree, the answer is not an artefact of any one of them.

whathowwhat it is blind to
Kernel Least squares for the output pixel as a linear combination of same-colour source pixels, free taps to ±5. Needs registration. Sub-pixel error smears the answer.
Noise Photon transfer. The slope of variance against signal gives Neff = 1/Σw², the effective number of photodiodes behind each output pixel. Registration entirely. Says nothing about where the taps are.
Geometry Sub-pixel phase between the G1 and G2 planes inside one frame. Any external reference. Only sees the sampling grid, not the weights.

For OG3K the three land on the same answer: the fitted kernel gives Σw² = 0.291, so Neff = 3.44; the noise measurement, which never saw the kernel, gives 3.48. One per cent apart.

The sampling grid gives it away

The cleanest evidence needs no reference frame at all. In a Bayer array the two green planes sit on a quincunx: G1 at (even row, odd column), G2 at (odd row, even column), so G2 is offset from G1 by exactly half a plane step in each axis. Measure that offset in each mode and the 6K still is its own control.

Nine tiles across the grille give OG3K a vertical phase between −0.24 and −0.26 every time. A value of 0.25 instead of 0.5 has exactly one cause: the R/G1 output row is centred between two even sensor rows and the G2/B output row between two odd ones, which leaves the two Bayer row phases one sensor row apart instead of two.

For a ÷3 mode this test goes quiet: with an odd factor a symmetric bin centres on a photosite, so binning and resampling both give an even grid. OG2K's 0.503 therefore proves nothing either way, and the kernel and the noise have to carry that mode on their own.

Even and odd output columns are mirror images. Convert the weights to sensor coordinates: R samples at 4t + 8.53 and G1 at 4t + 10.54 — 2.01 sensor columns apart. A uniform output grid predicts 2. Pair-wise binning predicts 1. A global misalignment cannot explain it, because a shift moves both channels equally and leaves the difference alone.

So each output pixel is a linear interpolation between the two nearest same-colour photosites, positioned on an evenly spaced grid. Charge combination cannot produce unequal weights, and certainly not weights that flip with column parity.

It averages, it does not sum

Every clip mode sits 1.32 EV below the 6K still at identical ISO and shutter — mean ratios 0.399 / 0.392 / 0.406 / 0.410 across RGGB, and the DNGs record BaselineExposure 2 against 0.678, a difference of 1.32.

The part that settles it is that OG3K and OG2K sit at the same level (127.1 against 124.8). If charge were being summed, ÷3 would be 2.25× brighter than ÷2. It is not, so whatever combines is normalised afterwards.

Two things worth sitting with.

The noise shortfall is small. A tenth to a third of a stop. “It is not really combining four photodiodes” sounds alarming and converts to almost nothing. The real cost is aliasing.

The weak axis is opposite in the two modes. OG3K's two-row average has an exact null at the source Nyquist — vertically it is a textbook ÷2 prefilter — while horizontally the two-tap interpolation still passes about half. OG2K is the other way round, and worse: a two-tap average cannot prefilter a ÷3 decimation at all, so the fold at ν = ⅓ still carries 0.5. OG3K moirés horizontally, OG2K moirés vertically.

Horizontal interpolation instead of binning is not pure loss: it keeps more horizontal detail than a true 2×2 would (0.79 against 0.707 at Nyquist). It is sharpness traded for aliasing, not sharpness thrown away.

The RWZM kernel, solved

Hbin, Vbin, Hbin2 and RWZM establishes from the firmware that FHD is the ÷2 sensor mode feeding the resampler at 1600 / 1024 = 1.5625×. That makes OG3K → FHD a measurement of the resampler with no sensor modelling in it at all — and both frames are in set one, so they align exactly.

r² = 0.99923. Sixteen phases solved separately:

  • The ratio is exactly 25/16 and the output grid is uniform. Across all sixteen phases the fitted kernel centroid tracks the predicted phase frac(25p/16 + C) with a constant residual of −0.41 ± 0.02.
  • It is isotropic. The two axes agree to ±0.01 at every point; Σw² is 0.5603 vertical against 0.5532 horizontal.
  • Neff is 1.78 and 1.81 per axis, so 3.2 in two dimensions.

A cross-check that costs nothing: push white noise through the measured kernels and read it with the same estimator used on the real files.

FHD inherits OG3K's paired row grid. The resampler assumes its input is evenly spaced, and it is not — FHD measures −0.362 where the ideal is −0.5 and OG3K is −0.248. The error is diluted, not removed.

The merge tuples were right all along

Every setting on the sensor lists four merge tuples across the 70 modes and says of them: “The last two are confirmed as effective X/Y geometry scale — but that proves a coordinate ratio and says nothing about whether charge is combined or rows are skipped. The first two are not pinned down at all.”

The first two are the per-axis tap counts. The pixels say so:

The lone 2 in (3,2,3,3) is the same “only two rows” the kernel fit found. That is not a reading of the pixels agreeing with a guess — it is a ROM table and a photograph, taken independently, saying the same number.

The measurement adds what the table cannot: vertical is a real average and horizontal is a phase-aware resample. The registers on that page (0x001C selecting 1×/2×/3×, the 0x0717 group holding twice the merge factor, 0x0058 enabling it) only carry a factor. None of them carries tap weights, which is what you would expect if the horizontal path is a fixed resampler rather than a combining circuit.

One thing the frames cannot settle. A kernel measured at the card says what happened, not which block did it. OG2K's horizontal [1 2 1]/4 is exactly the hbin coefficient record 0 documented in the frame-reduction page — but hbin has no ÷2 at all, and OG3K's horizontal is a two-tap with phase-dependent weights that no fixed three-tap record can produce. So at least OG3K's horizontal is not hbin. Whether OG2K's is the sensor's own merge or an hbin ÷3 wearing the same classic kernel needs the profile table, not more photographs.

Where this leaves 2K

If the ÷3 mode is only binning two rows, the obvious question is whether a true 3×3 can be built. Two answers, and the second is the useful one.

Vertically it might be possible. If the first two tuple numbers are tap counts, then (3,3,3,6) is a mode that already bins three rows, which would mean the hardware can. Horizontally it almost certainly is not, and it barely matters.

Here is what each option is worth. Aliasing is the incoherent residual — the part of the error that no sharpening can recover — against a Lanczos-3 ÷3 of the 6K still, measured on three regions:

  1. Fixing only the vertical gets nine tenths of it

    A → A2 takes grille aliasing from 31.4% to 13.1%, and a textbook 3×3 only reaches 12.3%. The remaining 0.8 points is [1 2 1]/4 against a box — not worth touching the horizontal path for.

  2. Even a true 3×3 loses to the cascade

    12.3% against 6.5%, and Neff 9 against 10.24. Route C needs nothing changed in the sensor: both of its stages already ship, and FHD is that cascade at a different second ratio.

  3. Doing it in one resampler step is the worst option

    A fixed 1.20-wide kernel asked to do ÷3 barely prefilters at all, so full read + RWZM ×3 is worse than OG2K is today on both counts.

The reason C wins is that the two stages are complementary. The sensor's two-row average has its null exactly at the source Nyquist, which makes it a perfect ÷2 prefilter. Then RWZM's fixed 1.20-wide kernel, running on a grid that has already been halved, is 2.4 wide referred back to sensor pitch — about right for the remaining ×1.5. The fixed width that is a defect at ÷3 is the right width for ÷1.5 after a ÷2.

Output is 3032×2021 ÷ 1.5 = 2021×1347, cropped to 2016×1344 — still open gate, full sensor width.

C's second stage is an extrapolation. The resampler has only been measured at 25/16, twice, by two independent paths — but both at that one ratio. “The prototype width does not change with the ratio” has no cross-ratio evidence behind it yet, and the whole table above rests on it.

Two things fall out. LibRaw's DCB defaults (dcb_iterations=0, dcb_enhance=false) are the worst of the directional algorithms because the colour refinement never runs; switching it on brings DCB level with AAHD. But a chroma median pass beats every change of algorithm, which is what you would expect when the aliasing is already baked into the file and no demosaic can undo it.

And OG3K wants its row grid corrected first, because every demosaic assumes a square Bayer lattice and OG3K does not have one. A fractional row shift on the CFA before demosaicing — even rows −0.125, odd rows +0.125 of a plane step — buys 3% for the directional algorithms and 8–11% for the simple ones. OG2K does not need it; its grid is uniform on both axes.

Two traps

  1. Do not solve RWZM against the 6K still

    Fitting 4K against 6K produces a convincing horizontal sharpening that is not there — Σw² scattering from 0.61 to 0.96 with negative wings at both ends of the tap window. Those two frames differ by more than a resampler. OG3K → FHD is the clean path, and it gives two axes that agree to ±0.01.

  2. The noise estimator over-reads when taps overlap

    A diagonal high-pass tile estimator assumes white noise. A resampler with overlapping support correlates it, and the estimator then over-reads Neff by 1.4–1.6×. OG3K and OG2K have non-overlapping taps so they are unaffected — which is exactly why their kernel and noise numbers agree — but every RWZM path needs the correction. This is the same effect seen from the other side in the frame-reduction page, where 3K footage shows almost no correlation between neighbouring same-colour pixels and UHD shows clearly more.

Still open

questionthe experiment
Does the RWZM prototype width change with the ratio? Everything about route C rests on it not changing. Record a clip through RWZM 1536 (1.5×) with an OG3K frame from the same position, and run the same sixteen-phase fit. Half-width still 1.20 confirms C; 2.4 means the kernel scales and B has to be recomputed.
Is (3,3,3,6)'s second 3 really three rows? The A2 row above is an analogy from the other two tuples, not a measurement. Shoot the 3×6 mode against a 6K from the same position and fit the vertical kernel. Three equal weights proves the hardware can bin three rows; two means the whole idea of a true ÷3 is dead.
Which register sets the vertical tap count? Diff the 161-register pattern of a (3,2,3,3) mode against a (3,3,3,6) one. They share a horizontal count of 3 and differ only vertically, so the difference should be a handful of registers — quite possibly among the eleven still undecoded.
OG2K deserves a better measurement. Its kernel came off the second set at r² ≈ 0.93. Reshoot OG2K and 6K in one session. Integer registration should take it to 0.99 and raise its detail to OG3K's level.
One camera, one firmware. Ver.5.02.0.V91 only. Repeat on a second body. The horizontal interpolation weights are the thing most likely to move.
